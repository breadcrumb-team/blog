If you sell or buy sponsorships, you already feel the tooling split: one set of products helps you find and price the deal, another helps you fulfill it. What still decides next year’s budget is something else entirely — the **sponsor recap**, the **sponsorship performance report**, and the **sponsorship ROI report** that prove the partnership worked.

That proof layer is the gap nobody owns.

## What is the sponsorship software stack today?

Modern sponsorship teams usually run on two strong layers:

1. **Discovery and intelligence** — find partners, benchmark pricing, evaluate proposals.
2. **Deal management and fulfillment** — contracts, inventory, tasks, deliverables.

Both matter. Neither fully answers the question a CMO or CFO asks after activation: *what did we get, can we defend it, and should we renew?*

## SponsorUnited: discovery and sponsorship intelligence

**SponsorUnited** is built for the front of the funnel. It helps brands, rights holders, and agencies discover opportunities, compare properties, benchmark deal value, and shape activations with market context and creative examples.

In short: it answers *who should we partner with, and on what terms?*

That is sponsorship intelligence. It is not a substitute for a finished sponsor recap or a shared performance report after the campaign ends.

## SponsorCX: deal management and fulfillment

**SponsorCX** is built for operations after the agreement is signed. It centralizes inventory, contracts, assets, and partner records; automates tasks and timelines; tracks activation progress; and supports reporting tied to what was owed and what was delivered.

In short: it answers *did we deliver what was in the contract?*

That is essential. A missed deliverable kills trust. But a complete fulfillment checklist is still not the same thing as a sponsorship ROI report a brand champion can forward internally.

## Where do sponsor recaps and ROI reports fit?

They sit in the **post-signing proof** stage — after discovery, after deal management, before renewal.

A strong **sponsor recap report** typically needs to cover:

- Delivered vs promised assets
- Reach, engagement, and audience fit
- Channel performance (social, broadcast, on-site, creator, email)
- Business outcomes where available (leads, redemptions, influenced pipeline)
- Clear renewal recommendations

A strong **sponsorship performance report** makes those results scannable for partnership and marketing leads.

A credible **sponsorship ROI report** connects spend to outcomes without forcing fake precision when attribution is incomplete.

Most teams still assemble all three from exports, screenshots, and end-of-season PDFs.

## Why the post-signing gap costs renewals

Three shifts make the gap expensive:

**Budgets face harder scrutiny.** Sponsorship now competes with performance media that can show a dashboard mid-week. A thin recap loses to a clear chart.

**Campaigns are more fragmented.** Creator deals, multi-platform activations, and hybrid live-plus-digital work multiply data sources. One wrap PDF no longer maps the work.

**Renewal is a sales motion.** The best teams treat the weeks after activation as the start of the next deal. That only works if the performance report arrives while the relationship is warm — and is good enough for an internal champion to share without rebuilding it.

When proof is late, thin, or one-sided, renewals stall and upsells die in committee.

## What “owning the gap” means for partnership teams

Owning post-signing proof is not another fulfillment dashboard. It is a different job:

- **A shared source of truth** for rights holder and brand, not two competing decks
- **Narrative plus metrics**, because nobody forwards a spreadsheet
- **Speed**, so a serious recap takes days, not a month
- **A path to the next ask**, with stop / start / scale recommendations grounded in the data

That is the layer between “we closed the deal” and “we renewed the deal.”

## FAQ: sponsor recaps, performance reports, and ROI reports

### What is a sponsor recap?

A sponsor recap is the post-campaign narrative that shows what was delivered, how it performed, and what should happen next. The best ones are renewal tools, not thank-you PDFs.

### What belongs in a sponsorship performance report?

Delivery against commitments, audience and channel results, standout activations, gaps, and a clear summary a non-analyst can skim in five minutes.

### How is a sponsorship ROI report different?

ROI reporting ties outcomes to investment. It should separate what you can prove (delivery, engagement, attributed actions) from what you can only estimate (influenced pipeline, brand lift), and say so plainly.

### Who should own post-signing proof — the property or the brand?

Both need the same facts. Properties that provide a credible shared report make renewal easier. Brands that rebuild everything from scratch every cycle waste time and weaken trust.
