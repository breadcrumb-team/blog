There is no single best sponsorship intelligence platform. The label now covers four different jobs: finding and pitching sponsors, measuring logo exposure and media value, benchmarking what deals cost, and reporting to each partner so they renew. Pick the tool by the job you need done first. If renewals are the pressure, judge every vendor by what your partner actually receives.

This guide is for heads of partnerships and partnership strategy at clubs, leagues, media companies and event owners. Maybe a sponsor just asked "what did we get?" and your team is rebuilding the recap by hand again. Maybe leadership wants a shortlist by the end of the quarter. Either way, every demo says "sponsorship intelligence" and they all start to blur.

Below we sort the market by job, compare the tools inside each group, and say plainly what each one does not do. Every cell in the tables comes from the vendor's own site, checked on November 2, 2026. Where a site did not say, we wrote "not stated on site".

## What a sponsorship intelligence platform is, and what the label hides

A sponsorship intelligence platform is software that turns sponsorship data into a decision. Which brand to call. What an asset is worth. What a deal should cost. Whether a partner is getting what they paid for.

The trouble is that the phrase now sits on top of very different products. SponsorUnited calls itself "the industry's leading sponsorship intelligence platform" and is mostly about market data and deal pricing. Foocus uses the same words for logo exposure measurement. Blinkfire calls itself a sponsorship data platform built on computer vision.

The label is also getting more crowded. In 2026, [Nielsen Sports South Africa launched SIYA](https://themediaonline.co.za/2026/10/nielsen-sports-sa-puts-150-million-sports-fan-data-points-behind-ai-platform/), short for Sponsorship Intelligence Yielding Answers. In October, Box Count and Data Agents [launched pep](https://sponsorship.org/box-count-and-data-agents-launch-pep-the-partnership-effectiveness-platform/), which they call a Partnership Effectiveness Platform. Same category words, very different products.

So the useful question is not "which platform is best?" It is "which job am I hiring this tool for?"

## The four jobs rights holders buy these tools for

### Finding and pitching sponsors

This is discovery. Which brands fit your property, who to contact, and when their current deal ends. [SponsorUnited](https://www.sponsorunited.com/) and [Vega](https://www.vegasports.ai/) lead here. SponsorFlo also covers prospecting, but as part of a broader sponsorship CRM.

These tools help you sign the deal. They are not built to report on it once it is live. That handoff is a known weak spot, and it is where a lot of proof gets lost.

### Measuring exposure and media value

This is the "what did the logo get" job. Computer vision finds brand appearances in broadcast, streaming, social and sometimes in venue footage, then converts that exposure into a dollar figure. [Nielsen Sports](https://www.nielsen.com/report/sponsorship-media-value-benchmarking-report/), [Relo Metrics](https://relometrics.com/), [Blinkfire](https://www.blinkfire.com/landing), [Zoomph](https://zoomph.com/), [Foocus](https://foocus.ai/), [Shikenso](https://shikenso.com/) and [BrandEye.ai](https://brandeye.ai/) all work here.

Media value is useful. It is also, by itself, what most sponsors are starting to push back on. The [pep launch announcement](https://sponsorship.org/box-count-and-data-agents-launch-pep-the-partnership-effectiveness-platform/) cites IPA research that found sponsorship is usually judged on exposure and media value, and that effectiveness data is mostly used after the campaign rather than to set objectives.

### Benchmarking deal prices

This is "what do comparable deals cost". [SponsorUnited's SPND module](https://www.sponsorunited.com/platform) is the clearest example, with pricing insight across deals, categories, markets, assets and entitlements. Vega says it produces a valuation grounded in comparables as part of the pitch.

It is easy to confuse price benchmarking with performance benchmarking. Blinkfire, Zoomph and Nielsen benchmark exposure and media value across partners and leagues. That tells you how an asset performed against peers, not what a brand paid for it.

### Reporting to each partner and building the renewal case

This is the job most "intelligence" pages skip. A partner wants one clear account of what they got: exposure, activations, content, fans reached and, where possible, business results. That is the [sponsorship performance report](/blog/sponsorship-performance-report/) and the [post event sponsorship report](/blog/post-event-sponsorship-report/) your team builds today, often by hand.

[Trajektory](https://www.trajektory.com/partner-intelligence), [KORE](https://www.koresoftware.com/), [pep](https://pep.works/) and [Breadcrumb](https://www.breadcrumb.ai/) all aim at this job, each from a different starting point. Blinkfire and SponsorFlo also produce recaps as part of wider products.

## How to evaluate a platform: the criteria

Use these to score any vendor, including ones not in this guide. They are also the columns in the tables below.

* **Primary job.** Prospecting, exposure value, deal benchmarks or partner reporting.
* **Built for.** Rights holders, brands, agencies, or all of them.
* **What the partner receives.** A shareable recap, a live dashboard, a PDF, or nothing partner facing.
* **Renewal support.** Signals that a deal is expiring are not the same as proof that value was delivered.
* **Data sources.** Broadcast, social, in venue, ticketing, CRM, brand tracking, your own files.
* **Plain language querying.** Can your team ask a question in plain English, and what is the feature called?
* **Valuation method.** A named method for exposure value, or none.
* **Pricing transparency.** Public pricing or quote only.

If you are still deciding what "value" should mean for your partners, start with how to [measure sponsorship ROI](/blog/how-to-measure-sponsorship-roi/) before you sit through demos. And if your partners want audience proof, decide early how you will share [fan data for sponsors](/blog/fan-data-for-sponsors/) without handing over a fan list. That choice limits which tools are useful to you.

### Who each tool is best for

* **SponsorUnited:** best for market data and deal pricing across the whole sponsorship market. Not a partner recap tool.
* **Vega:** best for sports teams that want scored prospects and timing on competitors' renewals. It does not report on deals you already have.
* **SponsorFlo:** best for smaller properties that want one CRM from pitch to renewal with public pricing. It is not a broadcast measurement provider.
* **Nielsen Sports and Relo Metrics:** best for a recognized, named media value figure for broadcast heavy deals.
* **Blinkfire and Zoomph:** best for social and broadcast exposure value with detailed, auditable reporting.
* **Foocus:** best for teams that want exposure data inside the AI assistants they already use.
* **Trajektory:** best for automated recaps across digital and non digital assets.
* **KORE:** best for large organizations that want partnership and fan data on one enterprise platform.
* **pep:** best for teams that want objectives agreed before signing and an evidence based renewal case, with consultants involved.
* **Breadcrumb:** best for per partner recaps from your existing data. It is not a prospecting database or a computer vision tool.

## One platform or a stack?

Most rights holders end up with a stack. In its [sponsorship CRM guide](https://www.getboomerang.ai/glossaries/best-sponsorship-sales-crm), Boomerang says most rights holders stitch together a market intelligence platform such as SponsorUnited or Nielsen Sports, a CRM or partnership platform such as KORE, Salesforce or Trajektory, and a relationship layer. That is Boomerang's view, and it sells the relationship layer, but the pattern is familiar.

Here are three stacks that make sense by team size. We have not listed prices because most vendors do not publish them.

**A small property with a handful of partners.** One CRM that covers deals and deliverables, plus a reporting layer that turns content and results into a recap. Add exposure measurement later if a partner asks for a media value number.

**A mid sized club or league.** A measurement tool for broadcast and social value, a reporting layer that combines that value with activations, fan data and sales, and a prospecting tool if new sales is a priority.

**A large organization with a data team.** An enterprise platform for inventory and fan data, a recognized media value provider, a market data tool for pricing, and a partner facing layer so each sponsor gets a recap they can read in five minutes.

Whatever the size, the gap is usually the same. Measurement tells you what happened. Someone still has to turn that into one partner's story before the renewal meeting. Our [sponsorship attribution evidence ladder](/blog/sponsorship-attribution-evidence-ladder/) shows how to stack that proof from exposure up to business results.

## Before you buy: what your data needs for plain English questions

Several tools now let you ask questions in plain English. SponsorUnited has [Surface](https://www.sponsorunited.com/platform). Foocus [runs as an MCP server](https://foocus.ai/) so tools like ChatGPT or Copilot can query it. Nielsen Sports SA built [SIYA](https://themediaonline.co.za/2026/10/nielsen-sports-sa-puts-150-million-sports-fan-data-points-behind-ai-platform/) on its fan research. pep says you can ask the data questions and see how each number was produced.

The interface is the easy part. DataArt's Martin Smith makes this point in [a recent piece on sports sponsorship analytics](https://www.dataart.com/blog/ai-sports-sponsorship-analytics-semantic-layer). He cites a Forrester finding that 76 percent of US B2C marketing executives who invested in sports sponsorships struggle to calculate their return. His argument is that an AI tool can parse any question, but it can only answer usefully if your data agrees on what "premium partner", "activation", "engagement" and "valuable fan" mean.

In practice, check these before you buy:

* **Partner tiers** are defined once and used in every system.
* **Activations** are linked to the right partner, even when one activation ran in venue, in the app, by email and on social.
* **Fans** can be matched across ticketing, CRM, retail and hospitality records.
* **Engagement and fan value** have agreed definitions your commercial team signed off on.
* **Permissions** are clear on what a partner may see.

If these are not true yet, any tool will give you a different answer each time you ask. That is not a vendor problem. It is a data problem you will carry into whichever platform you pick.

## Questions to ask in a demo

Take this list into every demo. It maps back to the criteria above.

1. Can I see a real, anonymized partner recap, not a dashboard?
2. What does the partner see and do on their side?
3. Which data sources connect without manual exports?
4. Can it use data we already hold, like ticketing, CRM and our own spreadsheets?
5. How is media value calculated, and can I see the evidence behind one number?
6. How long does one partner recap take after an event?
7. Who owns the data, and what happens to it if we leave?
8. Does renewal support mean expiry alerts, or proof that value was delivered?
9. What does plain language querying run on, and what happens when the data cannot answer?
10. What do pricing and setup look like for a team our size?
