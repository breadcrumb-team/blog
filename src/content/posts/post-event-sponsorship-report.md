A **post event sponsorship report** is the short, sponsor specific wrap you send after a live show, tournament, festival, or campaign window closes. It proves what ran, shows the evidence, names outcomes you can stand behind, and ends with a renewal note while the room still feels warm.

Think days, not months. For most properties, the useful window is about **14 days** after the event. Longer than that and your champion has already closed the story in their head, or rebuilt it without you.

This is not the same job as a full season [sponsorship fulfillment report](/blog/sponsorship-fulfillment-report/). Fulfillment is the contract ledger across a deal. The post event report is the fast wrap for one activation window. Same honesty. Tighter clock.

## What sponsors actually want in the wrap

Sponsors do not need another thank you PDF stuffed with crowd photos. They need a file a marketer can forward to finance without rewriting it.

A strong wrap answers five questions:

1. What happened (event, dates, audience actuals)
2. What we promised versus what we delivered
3. Where is the proof
4. What outcomes can we defend
5. What should we keep, change, or grow next time

If your photos outnumber accounted assets, you built a recap album. Go back and finish the audit.

## Send it within about 14 days

Timing is part of the product.

**Within 48 hours:** pull digital metrics while platform access is still easy (social exports, email, QR, ticketing codes).

**Within about 14 days:** send the finished report. Fast enough that memory is fresh. Slow enough that attendance, scans, and make goods are real numbers, not guesses.

Past a month, you are mailing a history lesson. Renewals get harder when the first serious proof arrives after budget season has moved on.

## Deliverables table (the heart of the report)

Open the contract. Give every promised benefit its own row. Status must be plain English: delivered, partial, missed, or over delivered.

| Deliverable | Status | Proof | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stage backdrop logo, both days | Delivered | Photo set + stage plot | Front facing camera angles covered |
| Booth space, 10x10 | Delivered | Floor plan + scan counts | Peak traffic Saturday afternoon |
| Opening remarks, 5 minutes | Delivered | Run of show + recording | Audience still filling seats; note for next year |
| Social package, 6 posts | Partial | Post URLs + reach export | One post delayed; bonus story already live as make good |
| Email footer, event series | Missed | Ticket platform screenshot | Ops miss; credit offered toward next window |

Optional value columns (charged versus realized) help when you have a method. Skip fake media value theater if you cannot explain the math in one sentence.

## Proof that backs the table

Proof supports the rows. It never replaces them.

Group it so a busy reader can skim:

- **On site:** signage, booth, stage, hospitality
- **Digital:** email, site, app, QR
- **Social and content:** posts, creators, livestream
- **Audience and ops:** attendance actuals, scans, redemptions

Caption every item with the deliverable name and date. One clean link or folder per sponsor beats a pile of unlabeled phone dumps.

## Outcomes without the theater

Separate what you can prove from what you can only estimate.

**Prove when you have logs**

- Attendance and scan based traffic near activations
- Promo code or ticket offer redemptions
- Email and site clicks tied to sponsor modules
- Social posts that actually went live, with platform exports

**Estimate carefully, and say so**

- Influenced pipeline
- Brand lift without a study
- Broad "media value" with no method

Sponsors trust a short honest outcomes section more than a flashy number nobody can defend in a CFO meeting. Save the deeper ROI narrative for a dedicated conversation; do not retell a full ROI essay inside every event wrap.

## Renewal note (last page, not an afterthought)

End with three bullets and a meeting ask:

- **Keep:** what clearly worked
- **Change:** what missed or underperformed, plus the fix
- **Grow:** where to invest next if the goals stay the same

Then propose a short call. The report earns the meeting. The meeting earns the renewal.

A soft close works: share the file a day or two before you talk, walk the table together, and ask what they want different next year.

## Annotated example: two day festival partner

Names are illustrative. Steal the shape.

**Sponsor:** regional beverage brand  
**Fee:** $45,000  
**Window:** two day outdoor festival

**Promised**

- Main stage wing branding both days
- Sampling booth with staffed pours
- Six owned social posts plus two creator tags
- Logo in ticket confirmation email
- Promo code for VIP upgrade, tracked in ticketing

**What the post event report showed (sent day 11)**

- Stage and booth: fully delivered; Saturday booth scans beat Sunday by about 40 percent
- Social: five of six posts on time; one late Friday; make good story posted Monday with strong saves
- Email footer: delivered across the series
- Promo code: 380 VIP upgrades; 54 percent first time buyers for the festival

**Renewal note that followed**

Keep stage wings and the VIP code. Move one underperforming mid afternoon social slot to a creator walk through of the booth. Grow sampling staff for the Saturday peak the scan data revealed. Meeting booked while the team still remembered the weekend.

That is what a **post event sponsorship report** is for. Speed plus a deliverables table beats a glossy wrap with no ledger.

## Build checklist before you hit send

- [ ] Cover names the sponsor, property, and dates
- [ ] Snapshot uses audience actuals, not proposal copy
- [ ] Every contracted deliverable has a row and a status
- [ ] Misses name a make good or credit
- [ ] Proof is captioned and tied to rows
- [ ] Outcomes split prove versus estimate
- [ ] Keep / change / grow bullets are written
- [ ] A meeting ask is on the last page
- [ ] The file leaves within about 14 days of the event
